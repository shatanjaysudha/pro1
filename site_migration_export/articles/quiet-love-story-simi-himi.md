# A Quiet Love Story

It doesn’t always begin with grand gestures. Sometimes, it begins with a question asked over chai, a soft voice remembered from family gatherings, and a businessman too busy for love — until he wasn’t.

How a Businessman, a Quiet Girl, and a 6½-Year Gap Built a Love That Chose to Stay

## 1 ─ Before the Question

I was 24.

Not chasing promotions. Not commuting to glass buildings. I was elbow-deep in a business I hadn’t started but now had to sustain — an inherited, responsibility-laden rice mill, with creaking machines, long ledgers, and days that never seemed to end. Every morning began with account books, calls from suppliers, and the slow throb of a dream I hadn’t yet dared to name.

Marriage wasn’t on my radar. It couldn’t be. Not when every hour had to be calculated — not just in money, but in effort, in reputation, in stability.

Still, family is family. And slowly, politely, the nudges came.

“Beta, business sambhal gaya hai… shaadi ke baare mein socha hai?”

Envelopes arrived, one after another. Photographs of smiling girls — in saris, in blouses, in borrowed confidence. The rituals had begun without me. I played along, nodding respectfully, telling myself, Not yet. Not now.

Then, one ordinary afternoon, my mother looked up from her chai and asked, lightly:

“Simi kaisi lagti hai tujhe?”

I paused.

Simi.

## 2 ─ The Name That Echoed

Simi wasn’t a stranger. She wasn’t even someone I had to Google. She was family — distant enough to marry, close enough to know. My cousin’s mama ki beti. She had been there, in the background of birthdays, Diwali dinners, and weddings. A soft voice, an easy smile, a presence I never truly looked at.

But that day — her name hung in the air. Not heavy, but certain. Like an unopened letter you suddenly feel like reading.

I didn’t say yes. I didn’t say no.

But the seed was planted — and suddenly, memory sharpened.

## 3 ─ Memory in Focus

That night, I didn’t sleep much.

Instead, I sifted through recollections like flipping through old photographs. Not digital ones — the real kind, curled at the edges and slightly faded.

The way she looked during that village festival last year — not flashy, not loud, just composed.

How she served prasad to children, her dupatta slipping and her fingers careful.

The way she greeted elders — eyes lowered, voice respectful — like she had been raised with gentleness that wasn’t performative.

None of these moments meant anything when they happened.

But together, in hindsight, they glowed.

## 4 ─ The Gap

There was a gap.

A 6½-year age difference, to be exact.

I had entered business early; she had just finished school.

In Indian families, age gaps are sometimes ignored. Other times, they become barriers.

And this one… felt both.

Would she feel too young?

Would people think I was in a hurry?

Would she want a college degree, a different life, time?

And yet — the louder question, the one I couldn’t ignore, was:

“If not her… then who?”

I wasn’t looking for perfect. I was looking for someone who felt like home.

And for the first time, someone did.

## 5 ─ That One Glance

We met again at a family function — a cousin’s engagement.

The usual chaos: DJ music, children throwing confetti, relatives comparing gold weights.

And there she was — across the courtyard, helping an old aunt with her pallu, unaware of how the breeze made her look cinematic.

We exchanged a glance.

Not dramatic. Not stolen.

Just quiet recognition.

Like two people realizing they were part of the same story and hadn’t noticed until now.

She smiled. I nodded.

And I knew — I would walk toward this, whatever it took.

## 6 ─ The Rooftop Decision

That night, I stood on the rooftop.

Below me, village lights flickered. Somewhere, a radio played an old Kishore Kumar song. Far away, the temple bell chimed once.

And in my chest — a calm storm.

I wasn’t a boy in love.

I was a man with responsibilities.

With a family business, a thousand unpaid errands, a quiet prayer for rain, and an unknown future.

And yet, despite all that…

“If I marry, it will be Simi. Or I won’t marry at all.”

The words weren’t loud. But they rang true.

And once said, I couldn’t unsay them.

## 7 ─ First Conversations

The next morning, I spoke to my mother.

She paused for a second, then smiled — that quiet smile only mothers give when they know something before you do.

“Toh bol Simi ke ghar walo se,” she said.

It wasn’t simple. Her family had their questions — as they should.

What about her studies?

What about the age gap?

What kind of life could I offer her?

I answered with honesty. No exaggeration. No promises I couldn’t keep. Just one truth: I respected her daughter, and I was ready to grow with her — not around her.

They asked for time.

And I waited.

## 8 ─ Her Voice

Days later, my phone rang.

Simi.

Her voice was soft — like flipping the page of an old book.

“Can we meet?”

We did — under gulmohar trees, away from noise.

“I didn’t expect this,” she said.

“Neither did I,” I replied.

She looked at me — not with fear, not with doubt. With thought.

“But when I heard what you said to my family… it didn’t feel like pressure. It felt like peace.”

And that was it.

No declarations. No dramatic lines.

Just two people who decided to walk a road neither had imagined — together.

## 9 ─ What Followed

Love wasn’t the hard part.

What followed was — opinions, judgments, whispers.

“She’s too young.”

“He has too much responsibility.”

“Will she adjust?”

“Will he give her space?”

But through it all, we stayed anchored.

We didn’t promise to be perfect.

We promised to be present.

That was our vow.

## 10 ─ The Wedding

Our wedding day came — simple, sincere, heartfelt.

No fireworks. No designer hashtags.

Just marigolds, relatives, and the smell of haldi and ghee.

She walked toward me with steady steps.

I looked at her and thought, This is the beginning of every good chapter in my life.

And it was.

## 11 ─ Today

Today, our lives are filled with more stories.

A home slowly built.

A child born on May 8, 2025.

Late-night feedings and early-morning account sheets.

Some days are hard. Some are harder.

But each day ends with us sharing a glance across the room — a glance that says:

“We chose this. And we would choose it again.”

## 12 ─ Why I’m Writing This

Not for applause.

Not for validation.

But because love like this deserves to be told.

Love that wasn’t based on dating apps, flowers, or filters —

But on real life, real work, and real patience.

If you’ve ever doubted timing…

If you’ve ever felt the world expects too much from you too soon…

If you’re still waiting for your story to begin…

Let this be your whisper:

It begins quietly. But it lasts loudly.

❤️

## Walk With Us, If You Feel Something Stirring

If our story sat with you quietly…

If it reminded you of a decision, a memory, or a hope you once tucked away…

Then we’re already connected.

We’re not chasing viral.

We’re building something slow, honest, and handmade — like good chai.

With love,

Simi, Himi & our little May-born miracle
